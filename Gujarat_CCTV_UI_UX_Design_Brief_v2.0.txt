GUJARAT POLICE INNOVATION CHALLENGE 2026
UI/UX DESIGN BRIEF — V2.0
Anti-AI-Template / Operational Command Center Direction

1. DESIGN DIRECTION
-------------------
• The website should NOT look like a typical AI-generated SaaS website.
• The design language should feel like a real government-grade command and investigation product: restrained, technical, spatial, information-dense and operational.
• Visual personality should come from the product itself — maps, camera feeds, timelines, evidence, system status and investigative workflows — not from trendy decoration.
• Primary goal: make the interface believable enough that a police operator could actually use it.

2. WHAT TO AVOID — AI-SITE VISUAL CLICHÉS
-----------------------------------------
• Do not use a pure-white background as the dominant visual identity. Use a neutral operational canvas with subtle tonal variation.
• Do not use rainbow coloring as a design system. Color should be semantic and functional.
• Do not use large generic drop shadows. Prefer borders, elevation through spacing, and very subtle depth only where necessary.
• Do not force three feature cards in one row. Use layouts based on information priority.
• Do not use dozens of emojis. Production UI should use icons and real data; emojis should be essentially absent.
• Do not make liquid glass a visual identity. Use normal solid surfaces; reserve translucency for a few overlays when technically useful.
• Do not use em dashes as a deliberate stylistic gimmick. Normal product copy is preferred.
• Do not use a fashionable font simply because it is fashionable. Use a highly readable UI font chosen for the final implementation.
• Do not put colored left stripes on every card. Status colors should appear only where they communicate state.
• Do not use fake testimonials.
• Do not build the interface out of repetitive bento cards.
• Do not use harsh gradients as decoration.
• Do not use generic purple/black neon AI branding.
• Do not use terminal windows as decorative components.
• Do not repeatedly use “It’s not X, it’s Y” copy.
• Do not use checkmark bullets everywhere.
• Do not include pricing tiers in the operational police product.
• Do not use decorative radial orbs.
• Do not use dot-grid backgrounds.
• Do not use sparkle icons as decoration.
• Do not use animated arrows everywhere.
• Do not add hover animations to every component.
• Do not use neon colors as the main palette.
• Do not make the interface pastel for the sake of being trendy.
• Do not intentionally omit Terms of Service or Privacy Policy if the public-facing website requires them.
• Do not use “no skeleton loaders” as a visual rule. Loading behavior should be selected based on context.
• Do not make the product look like a landing-page template.

3. VISUAL PRINCIPLES
--------------------
• Hierarchy over decoration.
• Information density over marketing blocks.
• Real-world spatial relationships over abstract cards.
• Operational clarity over visual novelty.
• Evidence over effects.
• Calm surfaces with strong typography and clear state changes.
• Color communicates status, not personality.
• Animation communicates change, not entertainment.
• Every decorative element must have a product reason.

4. COLOR SYSTEM
---------------
• Base: restrained neutral surfaces — warm/cool off-white, light gray, graphite and near-black.
• Primary accent: one controlled institutional accent, preferably a deep blue/indigo or restrained purple depending on the final brand direction.
• Semantic status colors: green for healthy/confirmed, amber for review/degraded, red for critical/error, blue for informational state.
• Use accent color sparingly for active navigation, selected camera, route highlights and primary actions.
• Do not use rainbow gradients.
• Do not use neon colors across the interface.
• Do not use pastel colors as a decorative theme.
• Dark surfaces may be used for command/navigation areas if they improve hierarchy, but the entire application should not become a generic purple/black AI dashboard.

5. TYPOGRAPHY
-------------
• Choose one highly readable production UI typeface and use it consistently.
• Inter, Geist or Space Grotesk may be used if they genuinely improve readability, but they are not a design requirement.
• Use typography hierarchy through size, weight and spacing rather than oversized marketing headlines.
• Operator-facing text should be concise and factual.
• Metadata should be visually subordinate but readable.

6. SURFACE / COMPONENT LANGUAGE
-------------------------------
• Prefer solid surfaces with 1px borders and restrained elevation.
• Use soft radius only where it improves grouping. Avoid excessive rounded cards.
• Use shadows only for floating menus, modals, popovers and elevated overlays.
• Use cards when grouping information is useful — not because every section needs a card.
• Tables, timelines, maps and split panes should be allowed to remain open on the page.
• Do not put a border and shadow around every element.

7. INFORMATION ARCHITECTURE
---------------------------
• Primary navigation: Command Center, Live Cameras, Vehicle Investigation, Alerts, Map, Evidence, Camera Registry, System Health, Administration.
• Primary global action: search a vehicle/entity.
• Secondary actions: filter, inspect, acknowledge, assign, export.
• The interface should guide users from detection → evidence → decision.
• Keep operational information visible without forcing users through marketing-style sections.

8. COMMAND CENTER
-----------------
• Use a functional operations layout rather than a marketing bento.
• Top row: global search, date/time context, system health, user/security status.
• Main area: live alert queue + GIS/map + selected camera/event.
• Secondary area: camera health, AI processing, recent investigations and system metrics.
• Use resizable panels or a grid only where it improves operator workflow.
• Avoid a wall of giant metric cards.

9. VEHICLE INVESTIGATION
------------------------
• This is the signature screen.
• Search field should be prominent but not oversized.
• Left: observation timeline.
• Center: map/route.
• Right: evidence and vehicle details.
• Route segments should reflect actual observation confidence.
• Camera nodes should show timestamp, camera ID/name, location and confidence.
• Evidence should be one click away.
• Use real-looking tables and timelines instead of decorative cards.

10. LIVE CAMERA VIEW
--------------------
• Use a practical camera wall/grid.
• Each tile should prioritize video, camera identity, timestamp, health and relevant AI status.
• Keep overlays compact.
• Do not add glass, glow, neon borders or animated decorations to every camera.
• Different states: LIVE, REPLAY, DEGRADED, OFFLINE should be explicit.

11. MAP / GIS
-------------
• The map is a core product surface, not background decoration.
• Use restrained map colors so routes and alerts are visually prominent.
• Camera markers should be simple and scalable.
• Use clustering at low zoom.
• Selected routes can use one strong accent color.
• Use side panels for filters and event details instead of floating decorative widgets.

12. ALERT EXPERIENCE
--------------------
• An alert must answer: what, where, when, why and evidence.
• Use semantic severity colors only.
• Primary actions: Acknowledge, Assign, Escalate, Close.
• Show confidence numerically or descriptively.
• Show source camera and timestamp.
• Do not use glowing red borders or flashing effects as the primary alert mechanism.

13. EVIDENCE / INVESTIGATION
----------------------------
• Evidence screen should feel forensic and precise.
• Show source, timestamp, case ID, operator, hash and processing metadata.
• Use a clean evidence viewer with supporting metadata.
• Terminal-style metadata may exist in a developer/admin diagnostic view, but should not dominate the public product UI.
• Reports should look like operational records, not SaaS marketing PDFs.

14. SYSTEM HEALTH
-----------------
• Use tables, compact status rows and charts.
• Metrics should be meaningful: camera availability, stream latency, AI queue depth, event throughput, database health, storage and network.
• Use status indicators with labels, not color alone.
• Do not turn health into decorative circular gauges everywhere.

15. MICRO-INTERACTIONS
----------------------
• Motion should communicate state change.
• Use short transitions for navigation, panel opening, filtering and selection.
• Hover may slightly change border, background or elevation.
• Do not animate every card.
• Do not use animated arrows as a recurring visual motif.
• Do not use pulsing effects unless the state is genuinely urgent.

16. LOADING / EMPTY / ERROR
---------------------------
• Choose the loading pattern based on context.
• For a table, use a compact progress indicator or contextual loading row.
• For a video connection, use “Connecting to camera…” with connection status.
• For a search, show query progress and result count when available.
• Skeleton loaders are optional — not banned — but should not become a decorative pattern.
• Errors should explain the problem and next action.

17. ICONOGRAPHY
---------------
• Use one coherent icon family such as Lucide if it fits the implementation.
• Icons should support recognition and navigation, not act as decoration.
• Do not place sparkle icons next to every AI label.
• Use familiar domain icons: camera, vehicle, map pin, route, alert, shield, database, server, search, evidence.

18. EMOJI POLICY
----------------
• Do not use the requested ~70 emoji treatment in the actual product.
• Production police software should use professional iconography and labels.
• Emoji can be reserved for internal prototype notes or optional empty-state experiments, but should not be part of the main design system.

19. MARKETING / LANDING PAGE
----------------------------
• If a public landing page is needed, keep it separate from the operational application.
• Do not use fake testimonials.
• Do not use fabricated government endorsements.
• Do not use fake customer logos.
• Do not show invented pricing.
• If deployment tiers are mentioned, describe them as Pilot / Regional / State deployment stages rather than SaaS pricing.
• Terms of Service and Privacy Policy should exist where legally/product appropriate; do not intentionally omit them.

20. RESPONSIVE DESIGN
---------------------
• Desktop-first because the command center is intended for large screens.
• At tablet widths, stack map/timeline/evidence panels logically.
• At mobile widths, prioritize alerts, vehicle search, camera status and evidence.
• Do not simply shrink desktop cards into a mobile column.

21. ACCESSIBILITY
-----------------
• Use sufficient contrast.
• Do not communicate state through color alone.
• Provide keyboard focus states.
• Use semantic headings and labels.
• Provide accessible names for icon-only buttons.
• Respect reduced-motion preferences.
• Keep touch targets practical on smaller devices.

22. DESIGN SYSTEM TOKENS
------------------------
• Spacing: 4 / 8 / 12 / 16 / 24 / 32 / 48 / 64.
• Radius: small and medium only; avoid excessive pill styling.
• Border: subtle neutral default; accent for selected state.
• Shadow: reserved for floating/elevated surfaces.
• Color: neutral base + controlled primary + semantic status palette.
• Motion: 120–220 ms for routine interaction; no gratuitous animation.

23. ANTI-TEMPLATE CHECKLIST
---------------------------
• Does this component exist because the product needs it?
• Could this card be replaced by a table, timeline or open layout?
• Is this color communicating a real state?
• Is this animation communicating a real change?
• Would a police operator understand it without marketing copy?
• Does this look like a CCTV investigation platform rather than an AI startup landing page?
• Remove anything that exists only because a trendy UI template usually contains it.

24. FINAL DESIGN CHARACTER
--------------------------
• Professional.
• Operational.
• Spatial.
• Evidence-first.
• Technically credible.
• Calm under pressure.
• Information-dense without being cluttered.
• Distinctive through product structure rather than decorative AI clichés.
• The strongest visual identity should come from the relationship between live video, GIS, vehicle observations, timelines, alerts and evidence.

25. DESIGN HANDOFF
------------------
• Figma component library.
• Desktop command center.
• Vehicle investigation flow.
• Live camera wall.
• GIS/map states.
• Alert states.
• Evidence viewer.
• Camera registry.
• System health.
• Administration/RBAC.
• Responsive layouts.
• Loading/empty/error states.
• Accessibility states.
• Motion specification.
• Color and typography tokens.
• Realistic mock data clearly marked as simulated.
• Separate public landing-page components from operational application components.
